## Executive Summary

AI has crossed from experiment to expectation in K-12 education. A September 2025 RAND survey found that **54% of K-12 students** now use AI for school — up more than 15 percentage points in two years. Teacher adoption has also surged: 60% of teachers reported using an AI tool in the 2024-25 school year (Walton Family Foundation-Gallup). A landmark April 2025 White House Executive Order mandated nationwide K-12 AI integration and created a Presidential AI Challenge. Simultaneously, the American Institutes for Research launched a multi-study AI in Education Network, and Stanford expanded its generative AI research repository to 1,278+ studies.

The debate is sharpening. On one hand, a June 2025 Harvard RCT (published in *Scientific Reports*) found students using a well-designed AI tutor learned **more than twice as much in less time** compared to active in-class learning. On the other, MIT's Justin Reich warns: "We're fumbling around in the dark," drawing parallels to past ed-tech failures. Critics worry AI shortcuts undermine critical thinking; proponents argue preparation for an AI workforce is non-negotiable.

The most outstanding experimental school model — **Alpha School** — embodies this tension: extraordinary claimed outcomes (top 1-2% nationally on MAP exams), but at tuition costs of $10,000–$65,000/year and with limited independent verification.

## Part A — State of AI in K-12 Education: Key Insights

### 1. Adoption Has Reached Near-Universal Levels — But Policy Lags Far Behind

AI adoption among students and educators has accelerated to levels that have far outpaced formal governance. Global student AI usage jumped from 66% in 2024 to **92% in 2025**, and in the United States, student use of AI for school-related purposes jumped 26% in a single school year, while educator use rose 21% over the same period (Microsoft 2025 AI in Education Report).

The most common student applications include research/content gathering (44%), summarizing information (38%), and creating study aids. For teachers, the leading use case is lesson planning (38%), and teachers who use AI tools at least weekly save an average of **5.9 hours per week** — roughly 6 extra weeks of reclaimed time over a school year.

However, governance has not kept pace. A February 2026 Coursera survey found AI adoption "dramatically outpaced governance," and as of late 2024, **only 31% of U.S. school districts** had any formal AI policy for students. At the state level, only 26 states had issued formal AI guidance for schools as of early 2025 (Stanford AI+Education Summit, February 2025). At least two states — Ohio and Tennessee — require school districts to have comprehensive AI policies.

> **Note:** The 92% global student usage figure comes from DemandSage/Microsoft aggregated data and should be treated as directional, not precise. It likely includes any AI use, not deep educational integration.

### 2. Federal Policy: A Transformative (But Vague) Executive Order

On **April 23, 2025**, President Trump signed Executive Order 14277, "Advancing Artificial Intelligence Education for American Youth." This is the most significant federal action on K-12 AI to date. Key provisions:

- Established the **White House Task Force on AI Education** (chaired by the Director of the Office of Science and Technology Policy, including Secretaries of Education, Labor, Agriculture, and others).
- Directed the Secretary of Education to **prioritize AI in discretionary grant programs for teacher training** under the Elementary and Secondary Education Act.
- Directed the NSF Director to **prioritize AI-in-education research** and create teacher training opportunities.
- Established a **Presidential Artificial Intelligence Challenge** to showcase student and educator AI achievements.
- Directed the Secretary of Labor to leverage WIOA funding and **increase AI-related registered apprenticeships**.
- Called for resources to be ready for classroom use within **180 days** of first announced public-private partnerships.

NVIDIA aligned with this EO by pledging AI literacy, credentialing, and educator enablement, partnering with Study Fetch and CK-12 to bring its Deep Learning Institute (DLI) teaching kits into high school classrooms. The American Federation of Teachers also announced a **National Academy for AI Instruction** in partnership with Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, and the United Federation of Teachers.

**Criticism:** The EO notably lacks specific funding allocations and detailed implementation timelines for underserved districts. It also comes in the context of Trump's December 2025 executive order attempting to **block state AI regulations**, which raised concern among K-12 education leaders about the removal of student safety guardrails.

### 3. Research Infrastructure: New Evidence Networks Launching

In November 2025, the **American Institutes for Research (AIR)** launched its *AI in Education Network*, a set of six coordinated studies aimed at generating rigorous evidence on AI in K-12. Studies include:

- **Teaching with AI**: Examining how professional development can help teachers integrate AI responsibly.
- **3D Learning with ADAPT-AI**: Testing human-AI collaboration in science assessment.
- **AI in the Classroom Testbed**: Benchmarking AI tools in real K-12 settings for evidence-based adoption.
- **Smarter Data, Stronger Schools**: Using AI-powered analytics to transform district finance and HR decisions.
- **LIFToFFS Model**: Evaluating AI-enabled personalized learning for rural K-12 students.

"As AI becomes part of everyday use in education, schools need more than marketing — they need evidence," said AIR President Jessica Heppen.

Stanford's **Generative AI for Education Hub** (SCALE/Accelerator for Learning) expanded its research repository to **1,278 studies** by early 2026, adding 204 studies in January 2026 alone. This is the most comprehensive searchable database of AI-in-education research currently available to practitioners.

The NSF also issued a Dear Colleague Letter in July 2025 inviting supplemental funding proposals from existing awardees for K-12 AI education activities, with three themes: teacher professional development, curricula/instructional materials, and technology/tools.

### 4. The Core Debate: Supplement vs. Supplant

Two fundamentally opposed camps are emerging:

**Team Supplement** argues AI is an inevitable part of the future workforce and students must learn to work alongside it. Carlo Rotella (Boston College) and Michael Horn argue that educators who use AI effectively will replace those who don't. "AI isn't going to replace teachers… but teachers who use AI will replace those who don't."

**Team Supplant** worries that AI's shortcuts impede genuine learning. AEI's Andy Smarick has called for a **two-year AI moratorium in public education** to prevent a rushed rollout. High schooler Ashanty Rosario wrote in *The Atlantic* about "AI Is Demolishing My Education," lamenting lost collaborative struggle with rigorous coursework. NYT columnist Jessica Grose argues "AI Will Destroy Critical Thinking in K-12."

MIT's Justin Reich strikes a cautionary middle note: *"We're fumbling around in the dark... AI bypassed normal procurement processes in education; it just showed up on kids' phones."* He draws on the history of ed-tech failures — from smartboards (no evidence of learning improvement) to early web literacy guidance — to urge humility. His MIT Teaching Systems Lab published *"A Guide to AI in Schools: Perspectives for the Perplexed"* (fall 2025) developed with input from 100+ students and teachers across the U.S., alongside a seven-part podcast "The Homework Machine."

A key unresolved challenge: **measuring AI-enabled learning loss**. "How does bypassing productive thinking with AI look in practice?" asks Reich. This remains an open empirical question.

## Part B — Outstanding Concepts, Schools & Tools

### Notable Schools & Teaching Approaches

#### Alpha School — The AI-Native Microschool Model
**Location:** Austin, Brownsville, Miami, San Francisco, expanding to Chicago, Atlanta, 35+ locations by Fall 2026  
**Tuition:** $10,000 (Brownsville) to $65,000/year (NYC)  
**Grades:** Pre-K through 8th grade (Alpha School); K-12 (Alpha High)

Alpha School is the most widely covered AI-native K-12 institution in the United States. Its model rests on four pillars:

1. **The 2-Hour Learning Model:** Students complete all core academics — math, reading, science, social science — in two focused morning hours via AI-driven adaptive software. No textbooks, no homework.
2. **AI Tutors as Primary Instructors:** An AI tutor provides 1:1 personalized instruction, assesses knowledge in real time, identifies gaps, and ensures mastery before progression. The AI-driven system is described as adaptive, not just content delivery.
3. **"Guides" Replace Teachers:** Adults in the classroom are called "Guides," not teachers. They do not lecture or grade; they provide motivational and emotional support. Guides earn six-figure salaries. An education degree is not required — only a bachelor's degree and background check.
4. **Afternoon Life Skills & Passion Projects:** The remaining four afternoon hours are devoted to workshops on public speaking, financial literacy, entrepreneurship, and project-based learning (e.g., designing, permitting, and operating a food truck).

**Claimed Outcomes:** Alpha reports students score in the **top 1-2% nationally on MAP exams**, and in a 2024-25 survey of ~1,000 students across 13 schools, **94% said they "love school."**

**Expansion:** Alpha plans to open in Chicago (Fall 2026), Atlanta, Puerto Rico, and North Carolina, bringing its total to 35+ locations.

**Critical Assessment:** These claims must be treated with significant skepticism:
- **Selection bias:** As MIT's Justin Reich noted, "It's strange to be comparing Alpha's students to students from a wide variety of very challenging circumstances, rather than comparing their students to other students in elite private schools." Students at Alpha come from families who can afford $40,000+/year tuition — a very non-representative population.
- **No independent replication:** Alpha's outcome data is self-reported. No third-party randomized controlled trial has validated the model.
- **Expert concern:** Emma Pierson (UC Berkeley BAIR Lab) noted, "There is evidence that AI could have exciting applications in education, but at the same time, we've seen educational experiments that do not work well for kids in the past."
- **AFT position:** "No matter how advanced, [AI] cannot replace the critical role of human educators. Real, consequential learning only happens when teachers and students collaborate in an atmosphere of mutual trust." (AFT Secretary-Treasurer Fedrick Ingram)

Alpha remains a fascinating experiment, not a proven model. Its most defensible claim is demonstrating high student engagement and motivation in a motivated, well-resourced population.

#### Jewish Leadership Academy (JLA), Florida — AI-Native from Day One
Opened in 2023, JLA was designed from the ground up to support AI-integrated hybrid learning, with Zoom Rooms, interactive flat panels, ceiling-mounted microphones, and auto-tracking cameras in every classroom. The school uses Panopto for lecture capture, and an approved suite including **Google Workspace for Education Plus, MagicSchool, PowerSchool PowerBuddy, Copilot, ChatGPT, and NotebookLM**. PowerBuddy guides students using the Socratic method; MagicSchool provides teacher-controlled personalization settings. JLA represents a thoughtful, institutionally governed AI integration as opposed to Alpha's AI-replacement model.

#### Harvard — Department of Physics / SEAS
- **Key Study (June 2025):** Kestin, Miller, Klales et al., *"AI Tutoring Outperforms In-Class Active Learning: An RCT"*, *Scientific Reports* (Nature), DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6
  - **Design:** Randomized controlled crossover trial, 194 students in Harvard's largest introductory physics course (PS2), Fall 2023
  - **Finding:** Students using a carefully designed AI tutor ("PS2 Pal," built on GPT-4) learned **more than twice as much in less time** and felt more engaged and motivated than students in active learning classrooms
  - **Crucial caveat:** The AI tutor was specifically engineered around pedagogical best practices — active learning, cognitive load management, growth mindset, and enriched prompts with pre-written step-by-step solutions to prevent hallucination. This is not "just ChatGPT." The result does not generalize to ad hoc AI use.
  - **Limitations:** Single course, single institution, no independent replication, focused on specific physics topics (surface tension, fluid flow), no long-term follow-up. These are significant limitations that should be stated plainly.
  - **Implication:** Well-designed AI tutoring can outperform expert human instruction — but the keyword is *well-designed*.

## Conflicts, Caveats & Uncertainties

### What the Evidence Actually Shows vs. What Is Claimed

**Strong finding:** The Harvard RCT (Kestin et al., 2025) is published in a peer-reviewed journal (*Scientific Reports*) and provides genuine empirical evidence that AI tutoring, **when carefully designed**, can outperform active in-class learning. This is not trivial — active learning is itself the gold standard. **However:** The study was conducted at one elite university, on a self-selected group of students who consented, using a bespoke AI tutor purpose-built by physics professors, not an off-the-shelf tool. The "5 percent problem" identified by Education Next remains relevant: tools can work in research conditions but only 5% of students may use them as recommended.

**Uncertain finding:** Alpha School's claimed outcomes (top 1-2% on MAP exams) come without independent validation and from a highly selected population. Self-reported engagement surveys (94% "love school") are not learning outcome measures. We should not conclude this model works broadly until it is tested with diverse populations and independent assessment.

**Conflicting data on teacher sentiment:** EdWeek and Microsoft report high enthusiasm among teachers (60%+ using AI). Pew Research Center found 25% of K-12 teachers believe AI tools do **more harm than good** and only 6% think they do **more good than harm**. These figures seem to conflict sharply — the likely explanation is that the Pew question asks about AI tools broadly/generically in education, while the Microsoft/EdWeek data captures teachers who are actively using AI for specific tasks like lesson planning. Both can be true simultaneously.

**Adoption ≠ quality integration:** High usage rates do not mean effective integration. "Schools that are exploring AI are integrating it in the simplest way possible, using it to automate administrative work or generate worksheets faster," notes the COO of Excel Education Systems. "But this isn't innovation; it's optimization of a model that's already outdated."

**The regulatory vacuum:** Trump's December 2025 EO attempting to block state AI regulations creates genuine uncertainty about student data protection. Several K-12 education advocates warned this leaves children without adequate safeguards just as AI tools multiply rapidly.
